# Spool target product contract

Target scope approved 16 October 2026. Spool is an application error tracking and crash reporting product for a US development team replacing Sentry, Datadog Error Tracking, Rollbar or Bugsnag (SmartBear). It serves the complete error-to-repair job: capture web/server/mobile failures, understand impact with source and execution context, route accountable team work, and establish whether a released repair held. PostHog Error Tracking, GlitchTip and Better Stack Error Tracking set the emerging-product comparison; the agent clauses go further through reproduction, tested source repair and live exposure-based verification.

This is the product being built toward. A clause is an obligation, not a claim that it ships today. The status table is based on current source/tests at `8f4d8fe3398de51567a0f0b04514c310e08547ba`, starting from the 16 October code audit at `9dc0f7ab3083035e56556fe6b773a443a843b3fc`. Changed code since the audit was re-read, including email destination management/testing, delivery issue links, setup/handoff guidance and the later workspace/packet read optimizations; the optimizations preserve the recorded feature boundaries. `done` means the stated bounded behavior has implementation and checked-in public-door tests; it does not turn local/provider-double results into hosted or commercial acceptance. `partial` names a working subset; `missing` has no implementation proof. ACCEPTANCE.md carries every clause's required journey and current status. Buyer review round 1 (17 October 2026) added SDK-006, TEAM-004, TEAM-005, SRC-003, CTX-003 and ALERT-005 and sharpened ALERT-002, ALERT-003, SRC-001, SDK-002 and CTX-002; their statuses were judged against the same source, and the later main commits through `f5acd4c` change hosting, demo and test-budget code only, not any clause status.

The first buyer is a US application business with several engineers, a web/server/mobile application, GitHub or GitLab, a delivery tracker and existing error monitoring. Prices, budgets and invoices are USD; US hosting/timezone/privacy and sales-tax expectations apply. Incumbent names identify switching obligations, not claims about their current plans or feature availability.

## Product separation and honest boundaries

- Spool owns error/crash records, release health, error-linked traces/replay, triage and the repair-verification loop. Mobile SDKs, native symbolication, provider applications, billing and heavy sandbox workers are required engineering, not reasons to reduce this scope.
- General work planning remains [Vector](https://github.com/just-every/run-vector), and rotations, SMS/voice escalation and incident response remain [Oncall](https://github.com/just-every/run-oncall) or PagerDuty. TRACK-001 and ALERT-002 connect those products; Spool does not recreate their broader job.
- Whole-platform infrastructure APM, general log warehousing, product analytics and model-evaluation suites remain connected services such as Datadog, Splunk and PostHog. CTX-001/002 and AI-007 still require the diagnosis context the error buyer needs; these boundaries do not exclude it.
- Scheduled-job and heartbeat monitoring is [Uptime](https://github.com/just-every/run-uptime)'s product. SDK-001 accepts Sentry SDK check-in envelopes (`capture_checkin`, `@monitor`, `Sentry.withMonitor`) at the DSN and forwards them to the buyer's connected Uptime heartbeat, linking the job's errors to it; without a connection they are counted as unrouted in capability diagnostics, never silently dropped.
- Cloudflare is the bounded intake/control plane, not a requirement to run native symbolication, replay processing or persistent sandboxes inside request execution. Use declared isolated worker/storage providers with quotas, custody, portability and erasure coverage. SDK-002, GOV-002 and AI-002 retain the full obligation.
- Production deployment remains the buyer's CI/CD action after review. Telemetry and model suggestions authorize nothing. AI-003 may write only an approved branch/PR; it never auto-merges or deploys. Replay/raw requests/identity are opt-in with preview and redaction; no blanket claim of perfect secret removal or legal compliance is made. Health/payment data requires buyer-approved agreements and controls before capture, rather than an implicit assurance.
- Local deletion cannot remove already downloaded archives, delivered messages or remote-provider copies; show the limits and partner deletion path. BASE-DATA-003 defines the exact offline-journal and backup limits. No unlimited-ingest or complete-capture claim is permitted.

## Named clauses

Existing ERR-001 through ERR-010 and BASE-DATA-003 identities remain stable. Expanded category clauses supplement their bounded guarantees; they do not turn the current single-owner implementation into a scope boundary.

### Evidence core

- **ERR-001 — Bounded admission.** Native version-1 JavaScript, Python and Go exception envelopes require active exact-project ingest credentials, registered release/build identity and bounded fields. Invalid, oversized, budget-limited and conflicting retries have explicit outcomes. Artifact URLs are never fetched. This native admission guarantee remains alongside the broader SDK target in SDK-001.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Send valid, invalid, oversize, foreign-project and conflicting-retry envelopes through public ingest; inspect the outcome and unchanged rows for refusals.

- **ERR-002 — Atomic acceptance.** Accepted means a sanitized occurrence, its group aggregate and its local new/regression alert commit atomically. Retry identity is project plus event ID and SHA-256 of canonical original accepted JSON before scrubbing: identical retry never increments, changed raw content conflicts even if its sanitized result matches. Raw bytes are not retained. Group algorithm version and basis remain inspectable.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Ingest an event twice, then change its raw secret under the same ID; inspect one occurrence and aggregate increment, one local alert and an explicit conflict without partial rows.

- **ERR-003 — Scrub before storage.** Apply the native allowlist, bounded text, query/fragment removal and sensitive-text scrubbing before persistence. Native envelopes contain no headers, cookies, request bodies or personal user identity. An optional random installation identifier supports bounded exposure measurement; it is never a name or address. Telemetry, source and notes are untrusted data and cannot authorize execution. Default protection persists when SDK-003 and CTX-002 introduce opt-in context; those captures require their own field policies and previews. Redaction does not promise removal of arbitrary semantic personal data.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Ingest privacy canaries and inspect stored events, issue views, packets, archives and delivery content; common secrets are removed while ordinary diagnostic content survives.

- **ERR-004 — Separated authority.** Every private API/document checks the current owner session or its exact ingest, deploy or read-only project grant itself. Cookie writes enforce same origin. Revocation and identity fencing take effect on the next request; confidential maps are never public. Ingest cannot read, deploy can only register releases/upload artifacts, and read-only agent grants cannot mutate. TEAM-001 extends this model to collaborative membership without broadening existing credentials.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Call each private transport anonymously and with wrong-scope, foreign-project, revoked and fenced grants; denied writes change no state, and direct map URLs reveal no private bytes.

- **ERR-005 — Source map identity.** Private flat Source Map V3 lookup matches project, release, build, debug ID and SHA-256 before presenting original coordinates. Missing, invalid or mismatched maps retain generated frames and a reason. Late uploads never rewrite immutable admission snapshots; current lookup is labelled separately. Lines are one-based, columns zero-based. No sourceRoot, section or remote URL fetching occurs.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Capture a minified second-origin exception before and after uploading the matching map; compare original coordinates, immutable admission diagnostics, mismatch refusal and confidential source exclusion from packets.

- **ERR-006 — Audited triage and regression.** Revision-checked open, acknowledged, resolved and suppressed changes are audited. A recurrence in a later owner-registered release reopens a resolved issue as a regression; delayed older-release events cannot invent one. Release ordering uses registered sequence, never guessed semver. Recognized JavaScript bundle hash suffixes do not split grouping; native anchors and reported frames stay immutable. Corrections retain exact occurrence provenance and reversible fenced apply/undo.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Resolve a captured issue, admit its fingerprint in a newer and then older release, and inspect regression identity. Preview/apply/undo split and merge through owner HTTP/UI, reject stale revisions and inspect original membership.

- **ERR-007 — Sanitized evidence packet.** A compatible v1 packet exposes issue/lifecycle/grouping, retained release sequence and per-event release/build/environment, generated/original coordinates, immutable diagnostics beside current lookup, links and coded coverage gaps. UTF-8 JSON is bounded to 256 KiB and ten events, removing oldest events first and naming included/available counts even if none fit. It excludes source snippets, sourcesContent and artifact bytes. It carries the latest computed repair observation and newest three owner-recorded repairs, with omitted history named. CLI and stdio MCP keep exact-project read authority and untrusted/no-execution labels; they cause no model egress.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Read the same packet through HTTP, CLI and official MCP transport at size/retention/import/loss boundaries; compare projections and verify no secrets, source bytes, writes or cross-project reads.

- **ERR-008 — Loss counters and repair observation.** Expose windowed accepted/rejected/budget outcomes and unknown pre-transport loss. Only the owner records revision-checked repair links, scrubbed notes, checks, approval/merge/deploy facts and an exercised-flow record. Links are HTTPS origin/path text and never fetched. Manual repair writes send no notifications or external work. Compute recur/reasoned inconclusive/no-recurrence-after-exercise from stored release sequence, maps, events, exercise and loss; list older-release events separately. Missing exercise, capture, map, exposure or retention yields inconclusive. Never label a quiet feed fixed. AI-004 adds rate-based live proof.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Record a repair through owner HTTP, deny every project-grant write, then exercise deployed/no-events, healthy exercise, older-release event, loss and recurrence cases; inspect packet verdicts and absence of side effects from manual records.

- **ERR-009 — Archive, restore and retention.** Versioned private archives carry stored source bytes, checksums, migration identity, triage, maps, repairs/checks, operator corrections, reproduction/fix/exposure history and erasure journal. Base owns staging, JSONL integrity, empty-target guards and atomic inactive restore; HTTP/CLI and legacy v1/v2 decoding share semantic validation. Run tokens, step leases, owner sessions, project grants, alert destination records/credentials and dispatch queues never travel; retained alert rules restore disabled and delivery history is historical. Older archives restore newly introduced collections empty under versioned rules. Manual cutoff retention removes private derived content, keeps repair snapshots with coverage gaps and exposes the deduplication horizon. Imported history creates no active grant, dispatch or current repair proof.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Export a populated installation via browser and rer, restore into an independent empty destination, compare retained records and inspect inert work. Refuse tampered, conflicting and nonempty-target imports before mutation; retain repair gaps after cutoff.

- **ERR-010 — Release gates.** A release must exercise real capture, symbolication, privacy, permissions, persistence and independent restore through the shipped app. Fresh setup has no demo rows. Private flows are accessible and responsive with explicit empty, failure and unavailable states. Source presence, local providers or a green build cannot substitute for hosted, mobile, integration or real-account acceptance. UX-001 and each connector clause specify the expanded product gates.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Run the named implemented journeys on the emitted Worker, inspect 1440/390 end states, then qualify each supported runtime/device and configured real provider with a buyer-controlled account.

### Teams and triage

- **TEAM-001 — Organizations, members and project permissions.** A US development team creates an organization, invites multiple simultaneous members, organizes projects by team, and grants owner/admin, billing (usage, budgets and invoices only), project maintainer, member and read-only access with least privilege. Removal revokes sessions, keys and linked service authority; service accounts have explicit project scopes. Private data, search, exports and integrations obey membership on every transport. Owner transfer remains available without restricting the organization to one active contributor.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Invite two teammates and an external read-only collaborator; grant access to disjoint projects, triage concurrently, remove a member and verify immediate denial across UI, HTTP, SDK-management, CLI and exports.

- **TEAM-002 — Accountable assignment and ownership rules.** Assign issues to real members/teams, import GitHub/GitLab CODEOWNERS at the exact linked revision, and define service/path/environment fallback ownership. Preview precedence and unmatched cases, route notifications to the assigned team, and notify assignees of due or unowned work. Assignment history survives member departure without conferring access. Rules can be tested and disabled by a maintainer.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Connect a repository with overlapping ownership rules, capture matched and unmatched errors, confirm accountable assignment and destination, then remove the owner and inspect reassignment/history without leaked access.

- **TEAM-003 — Collaborative lifecycle and bulk triage.** Teams comment, mention, subscribe (delivery per TEAM-005) and inspect an issue activity timeline; bulk assign, acknowledge, resolve, merge or suppress a selected search result with a preview. Resolution may be immediate, in the next release, in a named release, or from a commit/PR citation (TEAM-004). Explicit recurrence policy can reopen same-release repeats; old-release repeats remain separately visible and cannot fabricate newer-release regression. Suppression (archive) supports reason, forever, expiry/count/affected-user threshold, until escalating (unarchive when volume exceeds the issue's forecast from its own history, with the forecast shown) and snooze, with due follow-up and an audit trail. Concurrent edits refuse stale decisions.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Two members discuss and bulk-triage searched issues, resolve for the next release, exercise same/new/old-release recurrence, expire a suppression and push an archived-until-escalating issue past its shown forecast; confirm one accountable follow-up and stale-write refusal.

- **TEAM-004 — Issue short IDs and resolution from commits and pull requests.** Every issue has a stable human-readable short ID per project (for example `CHECKOUT-1A3`), shown in the UI, alerts, packets, exports and search; it survives merge/split as an alias. A commit message or a GitHub PR / GitLab MR title or description containing `Fixes`, `Resolves` or `Closes` followed by a short ID (case-insensitive) links that commit or PR to the issue as soon as SRC-002 receives it, visible on the issue timeline. When a release registered through REL-001 or SRC-003 includes the commit, or the merged PR's merge commit, the issue resolves in that release with commit, PR and author recorded. A later occurrence in a newer release reopens it as a regression under ERR-006; an occurrence in an older release cannot. Citations of unknown, other-project or inaccessible short IDs change nothing and appear in the repository link log with the reason.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Push a commit and merge a PR that each cite an issue's short ID, register a release containing both through the release API and through `sentry-cli releases set-commits`, and confirm both issues show the link immediately and resolve in that release. Capture each fingerprint in a newer release (regression) and an older one (no regression), and cite another project's short ID (no change, logged).

- **TEAM-005 — Per-member notification settings and issue subscriptions.** Each member chooses, per project and per notification type, what reaches them and on which personal channel (email, Slack direct message, Microsoft Teams chat). The types are workflow (resolution, regression, assignment, comment, mention), issue alert, deploy and weekly report. A member is subscribed to an issue by being assigned directly or through a team, commenting, being mentioned, bookmarking it or pressing subscribe; the issue shows why, and an explicit unsubscribe overrides automatic subscription until the member subscribes again. Workflow notifications go to subscribers other than the actor. Deploy notifications go to members whose commits are in the release. The weekly report is opt-in per member. A member can opt out of a project's personal alert email without disabling the rule for others; only an admin can make a rule's delivery mandatory, and the member's settings show it. Every message carries a settings and one-click unsubscribe link that applies before the next message.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Two members save different per-project and per-type settings. Trigger an assignment, comment, mention, resolution, regression, issue alert, a deploy containing one member's commit and the weekly report, and confirm each member receives exactly the messages and channels they chose. Unsubscribe through a message link and confirm the next qualifying event sends nothing to that member while the other is unaffected.

### Capture and release health

- **SDK-001 — Multi-language SDKs and DSN-compatible intake.** Developers capture automatic unhandled and manual exceptions using maintained browser JavaScript, Node.js, Python, Go, Java/JVM, Ruby, PHP and .NET integrations, plus iOS, Android, React Native, Flutter, Electron, Unity, Rust, Elixir and Kotlin Multiplatform. A guided setup command, comparable to the Sentry setup wizard, detects the framework, installs the SDK, writes DSN/release/source-map configuration as a reviewable diff and sends a test event. Publish framework setup for React/Next.js, Express, Django/FastAPI, Spring, Rails, Laravel and ASP.NET Core. Existing standard Sentry SDK applications repoint their DSN without rewriting capture code; their cron check-in envelopes route to Uptime as stated in the boundaries. Versioned capability diagnostics identify unsupported items and loss until full support lands; unsupported behavior stays an engineering gap.

  **Acceptance evidence:** For every named runtime, execute an unmodified Sentry SDK with only its DSN changed plus Spool setup, capture handled/unhandled failures and flush/restart; reconcile persisted issue, release and discard outcomes through the public UI/API. Run the guided setup on a fresh Next.js and Django app and send one check-in with and without a connected Uptime heartbeat.

- **SDK-002 — Native crashes and symbolication.** Collect iOS/macOS native crashes, Android crashes and ANRs, hybrid React Native/Flutter failures, Electron/desktop native crashes as Crashpad/Breakpad minidumps, and their device/OS/app version context under privacy controls. CI uploads dSYM, ELF/native debug symbols, ProGuard/R8 mappings and Flutter split-debug information through Spool's CLI or, unmodified, the Sentry Android Gradle plugin, the Xcode build-phase upload script, `fastlane-plugin-sentry`, `@sentry/react-native` and `sentry_dart_plugin` paths listed in SRC-003. Match UUID/build/architecture before symbolication, show original frames and inline context with explicit missing/mismatch diagnostics, and preserve offline crash delivery across app restart. Heavy symbolication runs in isolated bounded workers.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Build signed test iOS/Android and React Native/Flutter apps, crash and hang on real devices, relaunch offline/online, upload matching/mismatched symbols from CI through the unmodified Gradle plugin, Xcode build phase, Fastlane action and Dart plugin, and inspect one deobfuscated issue without duplicate sessions; crash an Electron app and symbolicate its minidump.

- **SDK-003 — Automatic breadcrumbs and private context.** Opt into bounded console, network, DOM interaction, navigation and server-operation breadcrumbs with category switches, sampling, per-field allow/deny rules and client-side before-send hooks. Default SDK capture excludes secrets and request bodies; adding pseudonymous user/session identity or approved request properties requires explicit project policy, preview and erasure coverage. Capture ordering and truncation are visible so investigation never implies a complete user history.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Toggle each instrumentation category in browser/server setup, trigger a failure with secret-bearing URLs/forms, and compare the occurrence timeline, before-send filtering, truncation and stored/forwarded privacy projection.

- **SDK-004 — User feedback attached to failures.** An accessible, localizable feedback widget lets the affected user submit a description and optional contact/screenshot against an event or independently. It asks consent, strips sensitive metadata, supports attachment review and abuse controls, and connects duplicate reports to the issue without publishing identity. The assigned team can reply through approved email with delivery state; withdrawal/erasure removes feedback and attachments across exports.

  **Acceptance evidence:** An end user reports a browser/mobile failure with optional screenshot/contact, a teammate triages and replies, an anonymous abuse attempt is bounded, and withdrawal removes the canaries from issue, storage and export.

- **SDK-005 — Crash-free sessions and release health.** Publish sessions, crash-free users/sessions, affected users, version adoption, crash/ANR rates and trends by environment, app version, device and OS, with explicit exposure denominators, sampling and capture loss. Define session boundaries and privacy-preserving identity. Compare rollouts/rollbacks against comparable cohorts; healthy sessions must be observed rather than inferred from absent exceptions. Export reports with their denominator and uncertainty.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Run known healthy/crashing sessions across two app versions and devices, vary sampling and offline delivery, then reconcile adoption and crash-free rates against submitted sessions and visible gaps.

- **SDK-006 — Ingest-time inbound filters and allowed domains.** A project maintainer turns on server-side inbound filters in the app, with no SDK redeploy: browser-extension errors, legacy browsers (by named browser and version), localhost and loopback, known web crawlers (maintained user-agent list), React hydration errors, ChunkLoadError and failed dynamic imports, client IP addresses and CIDR ranges, error-message patterns, specific releases and environments, and allowed `Origin`/`Referer` domains (exact and `*.domain` wildcard) for browser DSNs. Filters run at intake, before grouping, alert evaluation and billable usage: a matching event creates no issue, occurrence, alert or billable unit and is counted per filter as `filtered` in BILL-001 usage. Each filter shows its recent match count and a preview against retained events before it is enabled; changes are audited and apply to later intake only. A browser event whose `Origin` or `Referer` is not an allowed domain is refused with an explicit status and no CORS grant, protecting a public browser DSN from abuse. New projects name their default-on filters at creation, and each can be turned off.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Enable each named filter in the app, send matching and non-matching events through an unmodified Sentry SDK and native ingest, and confirm the matching ones create no issue, alert or billable usage and appear per filter as filtered in usage while the others are accepted. Send from a non-allowed `Origin` and `Referer` and confirm refusal without a CORS grant; disable a filter and confirm later matching events are accepted.

### Investigation and source

- **GROUP-001 — Explainable grouping and durable corrections.** Group occurrences stably across builds and runtimes, explain the fingerprint/rule version, support SDK fingerprints and project grouping rules, and preview future rule effects against retained events. Bulk split/merge corrections preserve original grouping, alert/repair coverage and undo provenance. New intake follows the chosen versioned rule; historical regrouping is explicit and cannot erase previous failure evidence.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Capture equivalent and distinct failures across builds, preview a custom rule, apply it to future and explicitly selected historical events, undo a correction and inspect alert rates, immutable originals and repair gaps.

- **FIND-001 — Search, facets and saved investigation views.** Search the entire authorized retained corpus by time window, message, stack/function, project/team, environment, release, severity, status, owner, tag and approved event properties. Facets, counts, ordering and cursor pagination reflect the full query. Save/share views and deep links without conferring access; exports use the same query and report bounds or refusals rather than silently truncating.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Ingest more than the current list limit across projects/releases, find an older off-page event with combined filters, save a view, open it as permitted/forbidden teammates and reconcile query counts and export.

Share permissioned reports and export CSV/JSON.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Open a multi-release issue and a team report over a known event/session set; reconcile totals, affected counts and triage durations, compare time zones, build a custom widget and saved event query that match the same totals, and verify a read-only export omits inaccessible projects.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Build equivalent minified apps with each named bundler, upload from GitHub Actions and GitLab CI with Spool's CLI and with each SRC-003 path using deploy-only authority, capture a real failure and check mapped frames; deny reads/writes outside artifact scope and show a failed/incomplete upload.

- **SRC-002 — GitHub, GitLab and Bitbucket source context.** Connect GitHub, GitLab and Bitbucket with explicit repository grants. Link stack frames to exact revision/file/line, ingest releases and included commits, read CODEOWNERS, and show suspect commits with reasons and uncertainty. When an issue first appears in a release that includes a PR/MR, Spool comments once on that PR/MR with the issue link, short ID and suspect reason. Handle renames, monorepos and revoked/private repositories without leaking source. GitHub App and GitLab application setup, credential rotation, webhook signature verification and read-back reconciliation are part of delivery.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Connect a private repository on each host, deploy a commit that changes the failing frame, follow its source link and suspect explanation, see one comment on the introducing PR/MR, revoke the grant and inspect immediate denial without stale privileged reads.

- **SRC-003 — sentry-cli and Sentry build-plugin compatibility.** A team's existing CI keeps working against Spool when only the Sentry URL, organization/project slugs and auth token change. Spool serves the Sentry API endpoints these clients call (chunk upload and assemble, release, commit, deploy and debug-file routes) under deploy-only project authority with the ERR-005 and SDK-002 identity checks.   **Acceptance evidence:** In GitHub Actions and GitLab CI, run each listed sentry-cli subcommand and each listed bundler, Next.js, Gradle, Xcode, Fastlane, React Native and Dart integration unmodified except for URL, slugs and token. Capture a real failure from each built app and confirm the release, commits, deploy, maps and symbols are registered and frames are original. Run an unsupported flag (named failure) and repeat an upload with an ingest or read-only token (refused, nothing stored).

- **REL-001 — Release and deployment history.** Register builds, commits and per-environment deployment start/success/failure/rollback with actor, timing and included commit range. CI retries are idempotent; abandoned starts become visibly timed out and notify their owner. Immutable release sequence governs regression, while deployment history records multiple environments, progressive exposure and rollback without rewriting past event identity.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Report started/completed/failed deployments from CI, repeat callbacks, abandon one, roll back production and inspect included commits, timeout follow-up and regression ordering for delayed old events.

- **REL-002 — Suspect change and release comparisons.** Connect first occurrence, reactivation and rate change to the release/deployment window, suspect commit and changed source; compare release cohorts, adoption and loss before attributing impact. Keep correlation distinct from a demonstrated cause and preserve older-release failures during rollout. An engineer can confirm or reject a suspect and record evidence for later agent learning.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Deploy two commits while retaining old clients, trigger first/regressed errors and an unrelated spike, inspect candidate reasons and comparable release rates, then reject a false suspect without changing occurrences.

- **CTX-001 — Error-linked traces, logs and request performance.** Capture OpenTelemetry traces/spans and error links across browser, backend and jobs, showing the failing request waterfall, latency, dependencies, correlated structured logs and approved RUM context beside the stack. Preserve trace/span IDs through intake, sampling and exports. Link or forward context to Datadog and Splunk with permissioned deep links and explicit data/minimization controls. This is error diagnosis, not ownership of their entire APM/log warehouse.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Exercise a browser-to-service-to-job failure with propagated trace IDs, inspect one correlated waterfall/log path and missing-sample labels, then open authorized Datadog/Splunk context and verify revoked export credentials stop forwarding.

- **CTX-002 — Privacy-controlled session replay.** Attach browser and mobile session replay to the exact error/session/time, with console/network timeline and trace links. Offer first-party capture, and link replays recorded elsewhere through the CTX-003 connectors. First-party capture has consent and sampling, default input/text/media masking, exclusion zones, retention and role-scoped playback. Replay never includes production credentials by default; access/export/erasure is audited and missing replay remains explicit.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Opt in on a realistic checkout/account flow, trigger an error, play the preceding path from issue detail, verify masked fields in stored replay and exports, then revoke access and erase the session across playback/artifacts.

- **CTX-003 — Third-party replay connectors.** Link errors to replays the buyer already records in PostHog Session Replay, LogRocket, FullStory and Datadog RUM Session Replay. Each connector uses a least-privilege buyer API credential and reads the session identifier that provider's browser SDK attaches to the Spool event, or matches by an approved session property and time. Issue detail shows the linked replay at the error timestamp as a permissioned deep link with provider metadata (start, duration, page); Spool never copies replay content. Missing, expired or inaccessible replays carry a reason. Revoking the connector credential or the viewing member's Spool permission stops new links and refuses opening existing ones from Spool; disconnecting removes stored replay references under BASE-DATA-003. Sentry Replay links imported by MIG-001 follow the same model.

  **Acceptance evidence:** For each of PostHog, LogRocket, FullStory and Datadog, record a session with the provider SDK beside the Spool or Sentry SDK, trigger an error and open the linked replay from issue detail at the error time. Revoke the connector permission and confirm the link is refused with a reason, and that no replay content exists in Spool storage or exports.

### Alerts and delivery

- **ALERT-001 — Filtered rules and real anomaly detection.** Configure new issue, reactivation, regression, frequency/count/rate threshold and crash-rate/spike rules by project/team, environment, severity, release and approved payload properties. Historical-baseline detection accounts for traffic, seasonality, sampling and missing capture rather than renaming a fixed threshold anomaly detection. Preview the matched history, owner, evaluation window and destination before enabling; deduplicate each rule/window transition.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Create filtered rules in the app, replay normal/burst/seasonal/low-traffic windows with loss, inspect baseline and threshold reasons, and reconcile exactly one alert per qualifying transition and no alert outside its filter.

- **ALERT-002 — Email, Slack, Teams, Oncall and PagerDuty.** Route actionable error alerts to email, Slack, Microsoft Teams, sibling Oncall and PagerDuty, with project/issue/release links, short ID, ownership, severity and recovery/update semantics, plus the inbound triage actions named in ALERT-005. Use named provider adapters and least-privilege account setup, signed inbound actions, rotation and revocation. Teams choose separate destinations per rule. ## Retained erasure and correction custody

The following detail is part of BASE-DATA-003, ERR-006, ERR-008 and ERR-009, not an exclusion of future team, SDK or replay work. It describes the current declared stores and exact-identifier erasure policy. Team membership will extend permissions while preserving owner-only destructive confirmation and a verified authority successor. Every new personal-data store must join the cascade before its feature is accepted.

### Project-root scope under BASE-DATA-003

Implemented at `c279cd2d1fc02acfbc1160201782935e5324ddef`; GitHub Verify run 38215335300 passed `test:project-erasure` on that head. The owner's explicit project-root default is adopted: owner-only project erasure with a count-only preview, typed confirmation (the exact project identifier and the previewed plan) and a completion receipt with counts and times and no personal values. It runs on Base main's erasure helper (`src/modules/erasure`), declared in `src/domain/erasure/declaration.ts`; one helper line carries a correctness fix not yet on Base (docs/PROJECT-ERASURE.md).

Erasure deletes the project and all project events, issues, releases, repairs, repair checks, reproducers and their runs and run tokens, fix workflows, their step leases and receipts, exposure reports, artifacts, admission counters, alerts, alert destinations, alert rules, cursors, intents and history, mail jobs and attempts, project keys and their dev-minted marks, browser origins and guards, Sentry DSN aliases, integration rows in the project's namespace, Sentry-import targets and project-related development outbox content. Every deleted row gets a Base portability tombstone and stored transfer snapshots are invalidated in each batch. A Sentry-imported project's import-kit custody (its source records and plan entries) and every original export artifact that holds the project are withdrawn in the same import-kit batch as the cascade; other projects in such an artifact keep their native rows, but that artifact's bytes become unavailable to them too. Completion is measured within five minutes at the retained-event cap. Manual retention's visible cutoff, occurrence identities, guarded tombstones and deduplication horizon are unchanged. One erasure runs at a time; an erased project's identifier cannot be reused, since its tombstone would refuse every later archive holding the new project.

Retained scope exceptions, each named in the preview and receipt: the installation owner, claim ledgers and authentication state remain because project erasure preserves installation authority; unrelated projects remain because they lie outside the selected project; project audit entries survive with only their kind and time, their project reference replaced by a random label shared only with the receipt, so a database holder cannot join them back; receipts keep counts and times only; portability tombstones are keyed hashes. Owner self-erasure uses the separate workflow below; project erasure alone does not erase its retained owner. Selective owner erasure remains blocked for shared import custody and unsupported or ambiguous attribution.

Older archives are refused by the installation's restore fence, including archives holding only child rows, and by a fresh destination that first receives the post-erasure archive, or a later archive given with `rer import --journal`. A brand-new store given only an older archive has no later journal and cannot recognise the erasure. Already downloaded archives, delivered mail, remote provider records, D1 Time Travel and backups, raw database imports and destination secrets (`ALERT_DESTINATIONS`) lie outside the local fence; erasure reports these limits rather than claiming their deletion. The public-door proof is described in [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md).

### Installation-owner scope under BASE-DATA-003

The authenticated owner can erase their own personal data through the native HTTP workflow in [OWNER-ERASURE.md](docs/OWNER-ERASURE.md): a count-only preview, then an explicit typed confirmation bound to the previewed plan, run on Base's erasure helper (`src/modules/erasure`) with the same resumable multi-step plan, portability tombstones and receipts as project erasure, declared in `src/domain/erasure/owner-declaration.ts`. The operation names only the signed-in owner; it cannot target another person.

Authority moves first. A real successor must accept an ordinary invitation; the first atomic step rechecks both parties' sessions, promotes the successor and retires the predecessor, so exactly one active owner remains and the successor signs in and manages normally. Without a verified successor the operation refuses with a plain reason and changes nothing. A change of authority or captured data before the first destructive commit cancels the untouched operation and releases its write freeze; committed partial deletion keeps the freeze until the final receipt and stays resumable.

Deleted: the owner's operator row, tokens, sessions, external identities, provider session data, step-up challenges, agent and deploy keys they minted, development marks, setup links, pending dependency approvals and their auth outbox mail. Project ingest keys keep ingesting and stay listed with their minter attribution cleared, and any key stays revocable afterwards. Inviting a different successor retires the earlier invitation and invitee. Other people's operators, sessions, grants and captured values are unchanged. Retained import custody, an identifier inside a structural key, and a display name shared with another person are explicit holds that refuse without mutation; prose that names the owner without an exact identifier, unknown aliases and encoded text are outside the declared scope. Receipts carry counts and times only.

Archives exported before the erasure are refused by the same restore fence as project erasure, including archives holding only captured child rows, by a fresh destination that first receives the post-erasure archive, and with `rer import --journal`. A brand-new store given only an older archive cannot recognise the erasure. Downloaded archives, delivered mail, identity-provider records, `OWNER_EMAIL` and alert destination configuration, D1 Time Travel and backups lie outside the local fence.

Owner writes admitted before the handoff: every owner write rechecks the writer's session inside its own D1 batch, before and after its statements (still unrevoked and unexpired, at the operator's current version, and that operator still the active owner). A predecessor request admitted before the handoff and completed after it is refused with `409 authority_changed` and changes no row.
