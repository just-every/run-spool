## Product separation and honest boundaries

- Spool owns error/crash records, release health, error-linked traces/replay, triage and the repair-verification loop. Mobile SDKs, native symbolication, provider applications, billing and heavy sandbox workers are required engineering, not reasons to reduce this scope.
- General work planning remains [Vector](https://github.com/just-every/run-vector), and rotations, SMS/voice escalation and incident response remain [Oncall](https://github.com/just-every/run-oncall) or PagerDuty. TRACK-001 and ALERT-002 connect those products; Spool does not recreate their broader job.
- For monitoring and observability, see RunEditRun’s sibling [Porthole](https://github.com/just-every/run-porthole). Whole-platform infrastructure APM, general log warehousing, product analytics and model-evaluation suites remain connected services such as Datadog, Splunk and PostHog. CTX-001/002 and AI-007 still require the diagnosis context the error buyer needs; these boundaries do not exclude it.
- Scheduled-job and heartbeat monitoring is [Uptime](https://github.com/just-every/run-uptime)'s product. SDK-024 accepts Sentry SDK check-in envelopes (`capture_checkin`, `@monitor`, `Sentry.withMonitor`) at the DSN and forwards them to the buyer's connected Uptime heartbeat, linking the job's errors to it; without a connection they are counted as unrouted in capability diagnostics, never silently dropped.
- Cloudflare is the bounded intake/control plane, not a requirement to run native symbolication, replay processing or persistent sandboxes inside request execution. Use declared isolated worker/storage providers with quotas, custody, portability and erasure coverage; SDK-011's attachment, minidump, profile and replay items and SRC-004's source maps and debug files stream there rather than being held in request memory, and the request-size limits SDK-011 publishes still bind. SDK-002, SDK-011, GOV-002 and AI-002 retain the full obligation.
- Production deployment remains the buyer's CI/CD action after review. Telemetry and model suggestions authorize nothing. AI-003 may write only an approved branch/PR; it never auto-merges or deploys. Replay/raw requests/identity are opt-in with preview and redaction; no blanket claim of perfect secret removal or legal compliance is made. Health/payment data requires buyer-approved agreements and controls before capture, rather than an implicit assurance.
- Local deletion cannot remove already downloaded archives, delivered messages or remote-provider copies; show the limits and partner deletion path. BASE-DATA-003 defines the exact offline-journal and backup limits. No unlimited-ingest or complete-capture claim is permitted; GOV-003 and SRC-004 publish the limited figures a buyer buys (retention, retained volume, sustained intake rate and artifact capacity), and no lower undisclosed cap may stand in for them.

### Evidence core

- **ERR-001 — Bounded admission.** Native version-1 JavaScript, Python and Go exception envelopes require active exact-project ingest credentials, registered release/build identity and bounded fields. Invalid, oversized, volume-limited and conflicting retries have explicit outcomes. Each project admits at most 120 outcomes a minute (`minute_budget`) and retains at most 10,000 events (`retained_event_budget`); over-limit intake returns 429 with `Retry-After` and a counted outcome. GOV-003 carries the target that replaces both limits with its named volume reasons and published sustained rate. Artifact URLs in envelopes are never fetched. Sentry error intake now implements SDK-009's default acceptance with no release or an automatically created release/dist; only its opt-in registered-sequence-only policy requires registration. Native version-1 intake keeps the registered release/build prerequisite. The broader SDK target is SDK-001.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Send valid, invalid, oversize, foreign-project and conflicting-retry envelopes through public ingest; inspect the outcome and unchanged rows for refusals. Exceed the per-minute and retained-event limits and confirm the explicit 429, `Retry-After` and counted reason, with no partial rows.

- **ERR-002 — Atomic acceptance.** Accepted means a sanitized occurrence, its group aggregate and its local new/regression alert commit atomically. Retry identity is project plus event ID and SHA-256 of canonical original accepted JSON before scrubbing: identical retry never increments, changed raw content conflicts even if its sanitized result matches. Raw bytes are not retained. Group algorithm version and basis remain inspectable.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Ingest an event twice, then change its raw secret under the same ID; inspect one occurrence and aggregate increment, one local alert and an explicit conflict without partial rows.

- **ERR-003 — Scrub before storage.** Apply the native allowlist, bounded text, query/fragment removal and sensitive-text scrubbing before persistence. Native envelopes contain no headers, cookies, request bodies or personal user identity; the `user` interface that unmodified Sentry SDKs send is governed by SDK-008's project identity policy instead. An optional random installation identifier supports bounded exposure measurement; it is never a name or address. Telemetry, source and notes are untrusted data and cannot authorize execution. The event fields SDK-010 keeps from unmodified Sentry SDKs (frame local variables, SDK-sent source context lines, exception values, tags, contexts, request, extra and thread stacks) pass through these scrubbers and SDK-003's server-side rules before storage, and default secret names are masked inside frame variables. Default protection persists when SDK-003 and CTX-002 introduce opt-in context; those captures require their own field policies and previews. Redaction does not promise removal of arbitrary semantic personal data.

- **ERR-004 — Separated authority.** Every private API/document checks the current owner session or its exact ingest, deploy or read-only project grant itself. Cookie writes enforce same origin. Revocation and identity fencing take effect on the next request; confidential maps are never public. Ingest cannot read, deploy can only register releases/upload artifacts and record deployment starts/callbacks, and read-only agent grants cannot mutate. SRC-004 carries indexed maps, `sourceRoot`, published capacity and opt-in source fetching. Spool never fetches a remote URL.

- **GROUP-001 — Explainable grouping and durable corrections.** Group occurrences stably across builds and runtimes, explain the fingerprint/rule version, support SDK fingerprints and project grouping rules, and preview future rule effects against retained events. Bulk split/merge corrections preserve original grouping, alert/repair coverage and undo provenance. New intake follows the chosen versioned rule; historical regrouping is explicit and cannot erase previous failure evidence. Stack trace rules honor the per-frame `in_app` flag and the in-app include/exclude options that unmodified Sentry SDKs send, and let a maintainer mark frames in-app or not in-app (`+app`/`-app`) and include or exclude them from grouping (`+group`/`-group`) by path, module, package, function and platform family (Sentry's `stack.abs_path`, `stack.module`, `stack.package`, `stack.function` and `family` matchers), previewed against retained events and applied to later intake. The issue view collapses frames that are not in-app, and grouping uses in-app frames when any exist; MIG-001 imports Sentry stack trace and fingerprint rules. The default rule groups over the whole exception chain SDK-010 keeps, processing each exception in turn as Sentry's default does. The same wrapper exception with different root causes therefore makes different issues. An exception group is grouped by its top-level exception and its children. Frames hidden by `__traceback_hide__` are left out of grouping. A message event with no stack groups by its message template. Fingerprint rules can match any exception in the chain (`error.type`, `error.value`), the message, the level, the logger, tags and SDK name, as Sentry's fingerprint matchers do. ## Retained erasure and correction custody

The following detail is part of BASE-DATA-003, ERR-006, ERR-008 and ERR-009, not an exclusion of future team, SDK or replay work. It describes the current declared stores and exact-identifier erasure policy. Team membership will extend permissions while preserving owner-only destructive confirmation and a verified authority successor. Every new personal-data store must join the cascade before its feature is accepted.

### Project-root scope under BASE-DATA-003

Implemented at `c279cd2d1fc02acfbc1160201782935e5324ddef`; GitHub Verify run 38215335300 passed `test:project-erasure` on that head. The owner's explicit project-root default is adopted: owner-only project erasure with a count-only preview, typed confirmation (the exact project identifier and the previewed plan) and a completion receipt with counts and times and no personal values. It runs on Base main's erasure helper (`src/modules/erasure`), declared in `src/domain/erasure/declaration.ts`; one helper line carries a correctness fix not yet on Base (docs/PROJECT-ERASURE.md).

Erasure deletes the project and all project events, issues, releases, repairs, repair checks, reproducers and their runs and run tokens, fix workflows, their step leases and receipts, exposure reports, artifacts, admission counters, alerts, alert destinations, alert rules, cursors, intents and history, mail jobs and attempts, project keys and their dev-minted marks, browser origins and guards, inbound filter and grouping policies, grouping proposals and their server-computed review snapshots, feedback, release metadata/builds/policies and issue-resolution boundaries, Sentry DSN aliases, integration rows in the project's namespace, Sentry-import targets and project-related development outbox content. Every deleted row gets a Base portability tombstone and stored transfer snapshots are invalidated in each batch. A Sentry-imported project's import-kit custody (its source records and plan entries) and every original export artifact that holds the project are withdrawn in the same import-kit batch as the cascade; other projects in such an artifact keep their native rows, but that artifact's bytes become unavailable to them too. Completion is measured today within five minutes at the current 10,000-event admission cap; the target is GOV-003's published retained volume, within 15 minutes for 150,000 retained events and at no less than 10,000 events a minute above that. Manual retention's visible cutoff, occurrence identities, guarded tombstones and deduplication horizon are unchanged. One erasure runs at a time; an erased project's identifier cannot be reused, since its tombstone would refuse every later archive holding the new project.

Retained scope exceptions, each named in the preview and receipt: the installation owner, claim ledgers and authentication state remain because project erasure preserves installation authority; unrelated projects remain because they lie outside the selected project; project audit entries survive with only their kind and time, their project reference replaced by a random label shared only with the receipt, so a database holder cannot join them back; receipts keep counts and times only; portability tombstones are keyed hashes. Owner self-erasure uses the separate workflow below; project erasure alone does not erase its retained owner. Selective owner erasure remains blocked for shared import custody and unsupported or ambiguous attribution.

Older archives are refused by the installation's restore fence, including archives holding only child rows, and by a fresh destination that first receives the post-erasure archive, or a later archive given with `rer import --journal`. A brand-new store given only an older archive has no later journal and cannot recognize the erasure. Already downloaded archives, delivered mail, remote provider records, D1 Time Travel and backups, raw database imports and destination secrets (`ALERT_DESTINATIONS`) lie outside the local fence; erasure reports these limits rather than claiming their deletion. The public-door proof is described in [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md).

### Installation-owner scope under BASE-DATA-003

The authenticated owner can erase their own personal data through the native HTTP workflow in [OWNER-ERASURE.md](docs/OWNER-ERASURE.md): a count-only preview, then an explicit typed confirmation bound to the previewed plan, run on Base's erasure helper (`src/modules/erasure`) with the same resumable multi-step plan, portability tombstones and receipts as project erasure, declared in `src/domain/erasure/owner-declaration.ts`. The operation names only the signed-in owner; it cannot target another person.

Authority moves first. A real successor must accept an ordinary invitation; the first atomic step rechecks both parties' sessions, promotes the successor and retires the predecessor, so exactly one active owner remains and the successor signs in and manages normally. Without a verified successor the operation refuses with a plain reason and changes nothing. A change of authority or captured data before the first destructive commit cancels the untouched operation and releases its write freeze; committed partial deletion keeps the freeze until the final receipt and stays resumable.

Deleted: the owner's operator row, tokens, sessions, external identities, provider session data, step-up challenges, agent and deploy keys they minted, development marks, setup links, pending dependency approvals and their auth outbox mail. Retired: the bootstrap and managed claim ledgers keep only installation, time and completion facts, so the installation stays claimed and cannot be reopened; auth audit actors become random unjoinable labels. Inert grouping proposals and their private review snapshots are deleted. Captured data: exact known identifiers (operator ID, email, display name, bound IdP subjects) are replaced inside project names, issue titles, event messages, breadcrumbs and context, map JSON, diagnostics, repair notes, links and checks, alert titles and development outbox JSON, leaving every other value in the row intact; they match only as whole tokens, and an identifier found only inside a longer word or address is a hold. Project ingest keys keep ingesting and stay listed with their minter attribution cleared, and any key stays revocable afterwards. Inviting a different successor retires the earlier invitation and invitee. Other people's operators, sessions, grants and captured values are unchanged. Retained import custody, an identifier inside a structural key, and a display name shared with another person are explicit holds that refuse without mutation; prose that names the owner without an exact identifier, unknown aliases and encoded text are outside the declared scope. Receipts carry counts and times only.

Archives exported before the erasure are refused by the same restore fence as project erasure, including archives holding only captured child rows, by a fresh destination that first receives the post-erasure archive, and with `rer import --journal`. A brand-new store given only an older archive cannot recognize the erasure. Downloaded archives, delivered mail, identity-provider records, `OWNER_EMAIL` and alert destination configuration, D1 Time Travel and backups lie outside the local fence.

Owner writes admitted before the handoff: every owner write rechecks the writer's session inside its own D1 batch, before and after its statements (still unrevoked and unexpired, at the operator's current version, and that operator still the active owner). This covers project creation and credential minting, key revocation, browser origins, triage, assignment, suppression policies, corrections and their undo, alert rules and delivery resolution, and repair records; retention and restore apply the same check to every batch they commit. A predecessor request admitted before the handoff and completed after it is refused with `409 authority_changed` and changes no row.


Operator correction extension: exact retained-occurrence previews, revision/occurrence-fenced apply and undo, immutable original grouping provenance, operator-label assignment, event-time literal suppression receipts and release/environment deployment manifests are defined in [OPERATOR-CORRECTIONS.md](docs/OPERATOR-CORRECTIONS.md). Future Sentry capture uses the active versioned project stack/fingerprint policy, with immutable admission snapshots; native version-1 capture keeps its default algorithm. Explicit split/merge corrections do not automatically become future rules. Deployment manifests preserve native registered-sequence admission and SDK-009's Sentry identity/regression policy. New tables are archived and project-erased; personal attribution in these immutable operator records causes an explicit owner-erasure hold.

Correction custody also applies to ERR-008 and ERR-009. Repair recurrence includes retained native admission membership, current membership, and exact occurrence membership during that repair's lifetime, even after further corrections or undo. Any applied or undone correction involving the issue leaves a `grouping_changed` coverage gap; without retained recurrence its observation is inconclusive, because later intake under its own grouping policy cannot establish the corrected group's coverage. Unaffected issues retain the existing successful-observation conditions. Retention follows each erased occurrence through its original, current and intermediate groups, withdraws those groups' historical alert headlines and local delivery copies, and cancels their pending delivery work in the same transaction. Current issue headlines come only from retained member occurrences. IDs/counts, repair snapshots, safe alert chronology, audit and restore fences remain; delivered mail and external provider records remain outside local retention.
